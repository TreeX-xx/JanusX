# Agent Note: JanusX download page

Status: implemented

## Problem

Visitors need to choose a Windows package and understand installation before exploring the application. Repeated download actions separated by feature cards obscure that task. A failed release request is also insufficient evidence that the first release is still being prepared.

## Decision

The public page uses a centered introduction with an immediate Windows installer action, an expanding brand visual, a two-column package section, installation steps, product captures and support links. The 2026-10-23 visual refinement follows the user-provided Aigocy reference: a neutral light canvas, restrained vivid red accents, self-hosted Urbanist for Latin text with system CJK faces, dark display headings and pill controls with subtle inset highlights. The opening background is original artwork: `assets/intro-atmosphere.svg`, a soft-focus glass composition with a quiet center for the message — a red glass sweep with one crisp specular edge enters upper-right and its dark end sinks lower-right, continuing the light direction into the dark scene below; a heavily defocused gray sweep and light haze balance the left. It replaced the earlier borrowed Aigocy `download-atmosphere.jpg`, which was copied from a third-party template and has been deleted; no third-party artwork remains on the page. The artwork is local and scrolls with the panel. Full-page grids, drifting background orbs and dark-scene dust have been removed to keep the page quiet. The translucent navigation sits inside the opening artwork, and the headline stays centered independently of four smaller floating tool cards (Claude, Codex, OpenCode and Pi; Pi was added 2026-10-31). Those decorative cards are hidden at widths of 1100px or less.

The introduction and dark brand panel share `--panel-gutter: 24px` and `--panel-radius: 28px`, with a 12px gap; on mobile the tokens become 10px and 22px. A soft red light at the right carries the background's direction into the dark scene. The desktop Janus scene retains `--hero-h: clamp(260px, calc(100svh - 562px), 436px)` and an intro compression tier for viewports no taller than 860px. Scroll expansion uses `--hero-end-h: calc(100svh - 32px)`, sticks at 16px and fits the workspace image without clipping. The compact breakpoint is `(max-width: 855px), (max-height: 768px)` in both CSS and `hero.js`; keep them synchronized. The intro's closing tags must stay balanced so its overflow does not contain the sticky stage. The particle capsule and wordmark retain slow compositor-only movement. The capsule is a standby figure: after settling it idles on its own — eye blinks, occasional double blinks, brief glances and, since 2026-10-31, a ring of light that travels once around the body — waking the canvas only for the duration of each gesture and sleeping again between gestures. Without JavaScript the fallback capsule (a solid light outline with brand-red eyes) blinks with a compositor-only CSS squash. Reduced-motion removes idle animation and the scroll stage. Both package cards share a light surface, aligned metadata and actions; the dark installer button establishes primary priority. The 2026-10-31 refinement borrows Aigocy's material rather than its assets: every section label is a gel pill (light gel on the canvas, dark gel inside the hero); the package cards, installation steps, capture frames and support panel share one clay surface token (`--clay`: inset bottom shade, inset top highlight, two soft drops) instead of 1px borders; the steps are cards with a faint CSS-counter numeral in the corner; capture frames are matted with a 10px surround; and blocks below the opening get a one-time entrance from `reveal.js` (headings tilt up, cards rise; nothing is hidden without JavaScript or under reduced motion). Product captures form text/image rows on desktop and stack below 855px. Mobile keeps the primary action in the first viewport and balances the subtitle across lines.

The brand visual has one particle capsule with two eyes, a plain JanusX wordmark and a neutral black background. Since 2026-10-31 the figure is solid rather than dotted: the body is an 18-unit stroke in the 520×300 figure space sampled every 2.3 units with jittered targets (stardust, not a lattice), every particle is drawn as a soft halo under a crisp core, the eyes are brand red (#ff4530, matching the wordmark's X) with a small specular glint, and the frame fits the capsule to 55% of the card width or 70% of its height, further bounded by the wordmark's top so the two never collide on short cards; at 1440×1000 the capsule is about 415×212px (a third of the card width) and leaves roughly 33k opaque canvas pixels at rest versus 5k before. The entrance is choreographed in three beats: a wave starting at the red-glass corner reveals a drifting dust field that fills the whole card (low-discrepancy scatter, figure marks plus dissolving motes); the same clockwise wave then gathers the field along swirl arcs into the capsule (quadratic Bezier with coherent vortex bend and smootherstep easing, fast flight leaving short warm streaks, large and soft in flight then shrinking crisp on landing — a focus pull), and the body flashes once from its centre as the last particles land while settling from 3% large; the eyes arrive last in their closed-lid shape and open once the body settles, waking the figure into its idle behavior. Scene labels, wordmark, tagline and footer fade up in a short stagger; the total entrance is about 2.8 seconds on every viewport size. After settling the particles respond locally to the mouse. A short sticky scroll stage expands the card to the viewport width and blends into the six-pane workspace: Claude, Codex, OpenCode and Pi terminals alongside native CPA and Markdown preview. The stage reserves the final card height plus 60% of the viewport height for the transition; scrolling back reverses the transition without replaying the entrance. Small screens and short viewports keep the entrance and scene but use no reserved scroll space; reduced-motion preferences and unavailable Canvas/JavaScript show the scene followed by the workspace immediately. Canvas work stops once the particles settle and pauses outside the viewport or in hidden tabs. The first-screen download action shares the package section's validated installer URL, missing-package state and release-history fallback.

Four buttons select the overview, terminals, CPA or document capture. Selection updates the image, alternative text, description, pressed state and original-image links only after decoding succeeds. Failure preserves the previous view and permits retry; the last requested selection wins. Images open at full size for readable details. Without JavaScript the overview and its link remain accessible. Mobile and reduced-motion visitors have the same view controls without the sticky animation.

The site remains static on gh-pages. Release lookup selects only explicitly named JanusX Windows x64 assets, checks their GitHub download origin, and shows actual package sizes and publication date. Missing packages link to release history. An HTTP 404 means no public stable release is found; network errors, timeouts, and rate limits display a separate unavailable state. Without JavaScript the release links remain usable.

## Alternatives considered

Reusing the existing feature-led page avoids layout maintenance, but leaves the primary download decision spread across two sections. Copying Orca's entire platform matrix offers consistent platform browsing, but advertises packages JanusX does not publicly provide. A framework-based site supports future routing but adds a build and dependency pipeline to a single static download page.

## Consequences

The page makes the primary installer available before the visual, followed by detailed package selection, installation steps and six current black-theme feature captures: four terminals, CPA, Markdown editing/preview, files/code, session history and external sessions. These replace the older GIF presentations of the sidebar, standalone Janus CLI, roundtable, blueprint and knowledge tools. This iteration demonstrates real interface states rather than operation playback; no page asset uses the old recordings. The copy states that CLIs require installation and their own login or model configuration for model tasks.

The four hero views were captured on 2026-10-19 by `scripts/capture-workspace.mjs`, using the sibling app checkout's clean CLI fixture and real Electron build at `.cache/readme-blueprint-build/main/index.js`. The app shell and CPA's own appearance control both use dark. Native CPA capture uses the attached WebContentsView bounds, rather than an empty renderer screenshot. Installed immutable CPA components are hash-verified; backend v8.0.32 and frontend v1.26.5 run in a new local instance with zero accounts/requests. No real account configuration is copied. Terminal text is unsent task drafts; the document is authored demonstration content. Four project panes and two tool panes are asserted before the overview capture, and Retry/error states are rejected. Keep the default 62% project width: narrowing two terminal columns too far caused OpenCode/Bun to crash during capture. Do not disguise that failure with edited screenshots.

`assets/workspace/capture.json` records the new asset hashes, dimensions, pane counts, CPA versions and app entry hash. Captures are staged until all four succeed. `imported.json` records the original raw PNG sources/hashes for three recent dark app recordings: tools/filetree, session detail and external-session transcript. The latter two use authored demonstration conversations; no model result or successful Resume is claimed. Source documentation is the app repository's [README showcase Note](https://github.com/TreeX-X/JanusX/blob/main/.agents/notes/desktop/readme-showcase.md). The historical product-tour Note link no longer resolves and is superseded here. These recordings use an existing build with unpinned dirty source provenance, not a claim of reproducibility from HEAD or a specific public release.

Re-capture from the app checkout with `JANUSX_APP_ROOT` set to that checkout, `SHOWCASE_ENTRY` set to its prepared Electron entry, `SHOWCASE_GIFTOOLS` pointing at temporary pngjs/gifenc dependencies, and `SHOWCASE_CPA_COMPONENTS` pointing at the installed component directory; run `node ../JanusX-gh-pages/scripts/capture-workspace.mjs`. This does not change the app's shared recording theme or the user's current session. Branch-local PNGs keep the page independent of app-branch asset URLs. All images declare dimensions and descriptive alternative text; feature images load lazily. The former `tour.js` playback script is removed because the page no longer embeds GIFs.

CSS, release handling, the hero and the entrance reveal are separate files to keep layout and failure behavior inspectable; `reveal.js` only toggles classes and is inert without IntersectionObserver. Package filenames must retain the builder's explicit x64 naming; additional architectures require explicit choices rather than silently changing the selected binary. GitHub API availability affects live metadata, while release-history links provide the fallback.

Run `node tests/download.test.cjs` with Playwright available through `JANUSX_PLAYWRIGHT` or the normal module lookup. The check verifies the seven PNG hashes and six-pane/native-view evidence, release success, missing packages, wrong architectures, empty releases, request failures, invalid URLs, no JavaScript, all six updated feature images and five viewport bounds. Screenshots are written outside the site when `JANUSX_SCREENSHOTS` is set.

Run `node tests/hero.test.cjs` with the same Playwright configuration to verify first-screen installer routing and failure fallback, particle settling and pointer response, idle blink gestures redrawing then sleeping again, expansion/reversal, all four view controls, failed-image preservation, mobile controls, live reduced-motion changes and accessible no-JavaScript fallback. With `JANUSX_SCREENSHOTS` set, it captures desktop, expanded, individual feature, mobile and reduced-motion views. Both suites passed on 2026-10-23 after the Aigocy visual refinement. The hero suite (nine checks) passed again on 2026-10-24 after the field-gather entrance rewrite; the download suite was not re-run for that change because release handling is untouched. Both suites passed again on 2026-10-31 (hero nine checks, download ten scenarios, no page errors) after the capsule material/scale rewrite and the clay, pill and reveal refinement. The design review that motivated that change, its three hero prototypes and its before/after captures live outside the site in the sibling folder `../janusx-design-review`; earlier preview captures are at `%TEMP%/janusx-design-refine`. Keep generated screenshots outside the published site.
